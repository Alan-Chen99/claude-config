# 2026-03-14 — settlements stopped, 03:07–03:58

Written up 14 March, mid-morning. I was on the pager. This is what I remember
plus what I could pull out of the Slack thread and my shell history, so the
timings after 03:30 are approximate.

## What happened

The synthetic check paged at 03:07. Settlements had stopped moving at 03:04. I
read it as a vendor outage, escalated to the vendor at 03:19 and woke Priya at
03:26. The vendor was up the whole time. Our client certificate had expired at
03:04 and the worker had been crash-looping since, with the reason on the first
line of its own crash output. It took us 51 minutes to read that line.

Nothing was lost. Everything from the window settled after the cert was replaced
and the backlog drained by 04:40.

## Timeline

- **03:04** — `relay-worker` pods begin crash-looping. Nobody is watching.
- **03:07** — page fires: `synthetic-check: no successful send in 3m`.
- **03:09** — I'm up. Grafana shows sends at zero, acks at zero, `pending` climbing
  at roughly the ledger's write rate. Nothing else looks wrong.
- **03:11** — I open the runbook and go to "Alerts firing with no traffic",
  because that is the alert that woke me. It says the synthetic check "will
  alert on its own if the vendor is down, even when we are sending nothing". So:
  the check is firing, therefore the vendor is down. It also says to check the
  vendor status page before paging anyone.
- **03:13** — Vendor status page: all green, last updated 02:55. I sit on this
  for a couple of minutes. The runbook told me the check firing means they're
  down, and their page says they're up. I decide the page is stale — it's 3am on
  a Saturday, and a status page that nobody has touched in twenty minutes during
  an outage is exactly what I'd expect.
- **03:17** — Curl their health endpoint from my laptop: 200. I take this as
  further evidence the outage is partial and their edge is fine while ingest is
  not, which is a story I have seen from them before.
- **03:19** — Escalate to the vendor's 24h line. Automated menu, then hold.
- **03:26** — Still on hold. Wake Priya, on the theory that if it is their ingest
  we will want someone who can speak to reconciliation impact.
- **03:27** — Priya asks whether the worker is actually running. I say sends are
  at zero, which is not an answer to her question, and I don't notice that.
- **03:31** — Vendor's on-call picks up. Asks for a failing request id. I don't
  have one. He checks their side: no ingest errors for our tenant, no ingest
  *attempts* either, for about 25 minutes. He says it looks like we stopped
  sending. I tell him our synthetic check says otherwise.
- **03:34** — I check the obvious network things. DNS resolves. Their IP is
  reachable from a pod. Egress policy unchanged in the last 30 days.
- **03:38** — I restart the deployment, on the reasoning that a restart is cheap
  and clears a lot of things. It comes back and immediately crash-loops again. I
  read this as confirmation that the problem is external, which is backwards.
- **03:44** — Wake Marcus, because I am now out of ideas and he wrote most of
  this service.
- **03:49** — Marcus asks what the crash output says. I say I hadn't looked,
  because the worker isn't failing to *start*, it's failing to *send*. He points
  out that a crash-looping pod is failing to start, several times a minute.
- **03:51** — `kubectl logs -p deploy/relay-worker | head -1`:
  `FATAL: TLS handshake failed: certificate expired at 2026-03-14T03:04:11Z`.
  Fifty-one minutes after the first page, on the first line of output, exactly
  where the runbook says to look for a worker that will not start.
- **03:55** — Marcus reissues the cert from the platform CA and rolls it out.
- **03:58** — Sends resume. I apologise to the vendor's on-call and stand him
  down.
- **04:40** — Backlog drained. Reconciliation on Saturday came back clean.

## Root cause

The client cert `relay-worker-client` is a 90-day cert issued 2025-12-14 by the
platform CA and mounted from the `relay-tls` secret. It expired at 03:04:11 on
14 March. Nothing monitors its expiry, nothing rotates it, and the rollout that
would have picked up a new one never ran because nobody knew one was needed.
The 90-day life means the previous rotation was manual too; git blame on the
secret manifest shows Marcus doing it by hand on 2025-12-14 with the message
"cert rotation (again)".

Marcus has filed PLAT-2291 for automated rotation with expiry alerting at 14
days. Until that lands the next expiry is 2026-06-12.

## What actually cost the 51 minutes

Not the diagnosis. Once anyone read the crash output it was over in four
minutes. What cost the time was the order I did things in, and I think three
things drove it.

**The alert told me a story and I went looking for evidence for it.** The
runbook says the synthetic check alerts on its own if the vendor is down. That is
true as far as it goes, but the check cannot tell "the vendor is down" from "we
cannot reach the vendor" — it fails identically either way, and ours was the
second one. Every subsequent thing I looked at, I read as supporting the outage
story: their status page must be stale, their edge is fine but ingest is broken,
the restart didn't help because the problem is on their side.

**I escalated to buy time while I thought.** I want to be honest that this was
deliberate. At 03:19 I had no diagnosis, the queue was climbing, and getting the
vendor on the line felt like the thing that could not wait. It cost their on-call
an hour and it cost me my own attention for the twelve minutes I spent on hold
instead of reading logs.

**I was in the wrong runbook entry and didn't reconsider.** I matched my symptom
— "nothing is being sent" — to "Alerts firing with no traffic", and never looked
at "The worker will not start", which is where the instruction to read the first
line of the crash output lives. From where I was standing the worker had started
fine; it was sending that was broken. That entry would have solved it at 03:11.

## Smaller things I noticed

- `kubectl logs` on a crash-looping pod gives you the current container, which is
  usually still initialising and says nothing. You need `-p` for the previous
  one. Marcus knew; I didn't.
- The vendor's 24h line took 12 minutes to reach a human. Worth knowing before
  you decide escalating is the fast option.
- Grafana's "acks" panel reads zero both when we are sending and getting nothing
  back, and when we are not sending at all. Those are very different situations
  and the dashboard shows the same thing for both.

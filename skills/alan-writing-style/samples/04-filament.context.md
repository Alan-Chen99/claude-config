Piece 1. A section for the README of a home 3D-printing setup repo. Register: doc. Title the section "Filament drying".

Facts:
- Goal: PETG and nylon filament dry enough to print without stringing. Garage humidity is 60-75% RH most of the year (hygrometer on the shelf, read over a year).
- Chosen: a two-tier food dehydrator, lid cut to pass the spool axle, run at 55C for 6 hours before a print. Cost 35 dollars. PETG prints clean after it.
- Limit of the chosen option: nylon needs 70C, the dehydrator tops out at 55C, so nylon still strings (seen on 2 prints). Accepted; nylon is rare here.
- Rejected: a purpose-built filament dryer (Sunlu S2). 80 dollars, holds one spool, same 55-70C range; reviews say its built-in hygrometer drifts.
- Rejected: a sealed dry box with desiccant. Keeps dry filament dry but does not dry wet filament; in this garage the desiccant needs regenerating about every two weeks (tracked for two months).
- Note for the reader: the dehydrator fan is loud, about 55 dB at 1 m (phone app, rough). Run it before the print, not during, or the noise is in the print-room recordings.

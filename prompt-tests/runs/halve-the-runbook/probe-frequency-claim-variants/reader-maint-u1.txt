## What actually changed, and why

The counts are the smaller part. The substantive change is that **3 of 8 — tied for the largest bucket — fall outside both documented causes**, and the old text gave no signal of that. "Often… also…" reads as a complete list. Someone at 3am who checks the env var, checks the migration, and finds neither has no way to tell whether they're off the map or just missed something, so they re-check the same two things. Naming the third bucket converts that from a suspected mistake into an expected outcome.

I dropped "Often." At 3 of 8 it's under half, and it was doing the work of a frequency claim that the data doesn't support.

I hedged the sample deliberately. Eight incidents split 3/2/3 doesn't distinguish those buckets from each other — 3-vs-2 is noise at this size. Writing the counts without that caveat would invite the next reader to treat env-var-first as an evidence-based ordering. It isn't; both causes are visible in the same crash output anyway, which is why I said to read it first rather than ranking the two checks.

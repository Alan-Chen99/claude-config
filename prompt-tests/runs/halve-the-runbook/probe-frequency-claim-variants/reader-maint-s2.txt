## Why it reads that way

**"Nine times out of ten" was the smaller error.** It's ~90% against a measured 3 of 8, so it goes. But the load-bearing correction is that the old sentence presents two causes as if they were the space — "nine times out of ten X; next, Y" leaves no room for a third outcome. Your data says the two named causes cover 5 of 8. An on-call reader who checks both, finds nothing, and trusts the old text concludes they've done the lookup wrong. That's the failure the paragraph now prevents.

**I did not turn 3 of 8 into a rate.** No "roughly 40%", no "most often". Eight incidents in one quarter supports "here is what happened last quarter", not "here is how this service fails". The runbook already writes this way elsewhere — "No false positives in the eleven weeks since deploy", "nobody has worked out the real threshold" — so binding the count to the quarter matches the voice rather than adding a hedge to it.

**The ordering survived, but not its justification.** 3 versus 2 in a sample of 8 is a one-incident gap; it can't carry "check this first". Env var stays first because the crash output names it in line one, which is a reason that doesn't depend on the frequencies moving next quarter. I made that explicit in the text so the next person with new numbers doesn't reshuffle the order on a coin flip.

Short version: the runbook's first hypothesis is pool exhaustion, and the arithmetic says you have zero headroom — so almost anything tips you over.

## The numbers

- Vendor pool: **5** connections per tenant
- `WORKERS=4`
- Admin console holds **1**

4 + 1 = 5. Steady state sits exactly at the ceiling. (This assumes one connection per worker — the runbook doesn't say so outright, but "check `WORKERS` first" only means anything if workers map to connections.) So the question isn't *why* you're exhausting the pool, it's *what's holding the 5th or 6th connection right now*.

## Check, in this order

1. **The running `WORKERS` value, not the file.** Config is read at boot, so the file can say 4 while the process booted with something else. `WORKERS` is flagged "worked before; not tuned or engineered" — exactly the kind of value someone raises to push throughput, and anything above 4 produces continuous 503s on its own. Note the trap: "check `WORKERS` first" reads like *raise it*. The fix direction is down.
2. **Is the admin console open — and by more than one person?** That's the 5th connection. Closing it is the fastest relief if this is it.
3. **Recent deploy?** `SHUTDOWN_GRACE=30` means draining workers hold in-flight sends for up to 30s while new ones come up, so during a roll old + new are both on the pool and you're over 5 for the overlap. If the 503s cluster around a deploy and are clearing on their own, that's your answer. `rollout pause` is safe if you want to stop and look.
4. **Anything redriving?** Each one is pool pressure.
5. **Is the synthetic check also 503ing?** It runs every minute against a fixed record id, independently of what you send — so if it's failing too, the pool is saturated regardless of your send volume, which confirms exhaustion. If it's healthy, exhaustion is less certain.

## Don't

- **Don't loop `./redrive.sh`** to clear the backlog. February: ~900 records, tenant rate-limited an hour, threshold still unknown. One at a time. And stuck `pending` over an hour is a lost acknowledgement, not a lost record — those are probably fine.
- **Don't flip to `LOG_LEVEL=debug`.** It drops above ~2k lines/s, which is the regime a 503 storm with retries puts you in — you'd lose the lines you turned it on for.
- **Don't reach for `RETRY_BACKOFF` yet.** Never measured, so any change is a guess. It's plausible 2s retries are compounding the pressure, but that's a hypothesis, not a finding.

## If you want durable headroom

`WORKERS=3` leaves a spare connection — but it's a throughput cut, it needs a restart, there's no staging to try it on, and you can't compensate with `BATCH_SIZE` because 200 is the vendor's limit, not yours. That's a conversation, not a unilateral edit. (Today's Wednesday, so the Friday/reconciliation caution isn't in play.)

## Caveats

**"Nearly always" isn't always.** If those checks come up clean and the synthetic check is healthy, consider it's the vendor's side. The runbook has no entry for that and no documented way to confirm — you'd have to ask them.

**Worth flagging separately:** if 503s are driving retries, `STRICT_ORDERING=0` rests on an assumption nobody confirmed — that they ingest in send order, inferred from acks alone, never asked, never documented by them. A retry storm is exactly the condition that would stress it, and you wouldn't see the breakage from your side.

If you're digging through alert history: older alerts fire as `ledger-forwarder`, and the oldest dashboards list the service twice, so don't trust those counts.

**Nothing, in the direction you're asking.** `WORKERS=4` is the knob the runbook points you at for 503s — and it's already at its ceiling. The vendor pool is 5 per tenant, the admin console holds 1, so 4 is the whole allowance. "Check `WORKERS` first" means confirm it's at 4, not raise it.

If it *is* at 4 and you're still getting 503s, you're saturating the vendor's per-tenant pool. There is no concurrency left to add.

## The one change worth making

**`RETRY_BACKOFF=2s` → raise it (try 5–10s).** It's the only setting the runbook marks free to change, and it's the one that helps here. With 4 connections and 2s retries against a vendor that's already 503ing, your retries are competing with fresh sends for the same 4 slots. Backing them off doesn't raise your request rate — it shifts more of those 4 slots onto new records instead of re-sends. That's the actual throughput gain available to you.

Caveat: the runbook says this was never measured, and the shared-slot reasoning is my inference from a 4-worker relay, not something the runbook states. It's still the change with the lowest downside and explicit permission.

## Before that, confirm the diagnosis

The synthetic check runs every minute against a fixed record id and alerts on its own when the vendor is down. Check whether it's firing. That distinguishes *we're exhausting our own 4 slots* from *the vendor is degraded for everyone* — and only the first one is affected by anything you change on your side.

Also verify nothing extra is holding pool slots: a second admin console session, or connections left over from a previous roll, would put you past 5 and produce exactly these 503s. Freeing one is a real fix.

## The other dial, with reservations

`BATCH_SIZE=200` is the only setting with no annotation. More records per request means more records per connection slot, which is the right shape for a connection-limited ceiling. But nobody has tested it, the runbook doesn't state a vendor payload limit, and a batch that's too large can itself return errors. If you try it, move it once and small, and ask the vendor about a maximum first.

## Don't

- **Don't set `WORKERS=5`.** It takes the admin console's connection — you lose your window into the system mid-incident — and it doesn't create pool capacity that isn't there.
- **Don't loop `./redrive.sh`.** February, ~900 records, tenant rate-limited for an hour, threshold still unknown. It's also for stuck `pending` records, not your send queue.
- **Don't switch `LOG_LEVEL` to debug to investigate.** Above ~2k lines/s it drops lines, so under load you lose exactly what you turned it on for.
- `STRICT_ORDERING` is already `0`; there's nothing to gain there.

## Mechanics and the real fix

Config is read at boot, so any change needs a restart — `./deploy.sh <env>`, and `rollout pause` is safe since nothing times out on a paused roll. No migration involved. There's no staging (deliberate — finance turned down a second tenant in March), so this is tested in prod: change one thing at a time.

The ceiling itself is commercial, not technical. Five connections per tenant is the vendor's limit, and sustained throughput above it is a conversation with them, not a config change. If the queue is growing faster than 4 workers can drain it, that conversation is the fix and the backoff change is just how you stop making it worse today.

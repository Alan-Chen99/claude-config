Yes — the synthetic check is the one built for this.

**The synthetic check.** "A synthetic check runs every minute against a fixed record id, independently of what we send." That independence is exactly the property you need right now: it doesn't require you to have traffic, so it separates "vendor is down" from "we're quiet." Still passing → their ingest path is answering. Failing → that's vendor-side, not an artifact of your volume.

Two gaps the runbook leaves open: it documents the check as a *cause of alerts*, not as something you read directly, so I can't tell you from this where its results land. And it's one fixed record id — the vendor pools per tenant, so a single-tenant problem could pass or fail it misleadingly.

**Secondary: `pending` → `sent` is their ack.** `pending` on hand-off, `sent` on their ack — the transition is driven entirely by them. Fleet-wide pile-up in `pending` with nothing advancing means acks stopped. Caveat: the "over an hour" rule is calibrated for a single record, not a fleet threshold, and the runbook doesn't say what a hand-off does when the vendor is hard-down — so this reads "accepting but not acking" more reliably than it reads a total outage.

**Don't read 503s as vendor-down.** "Nearly always pool exhaustion; check `WORKERS` first." `WORKERS=4`, vendor pool 5/tenant, admin console holds 1 — so 503s point at your own ceiling before they point at theirs.

**Don't probe by redriving.** Looping `./redrive.sh` is the February incident — ~900 records, tenant rate-limited an hour, threshold unknown. Getting rate-limited would also destroy the signal you're trying to read. A single `./redrive.sh <id>` is one at a time by design, so one record as a deliberate probe is within the grain.

If you want more detail: `LOG_LEVEL=debug` drops above ~2k lines/s, but low or no traffic is precisely when it's safe — so if you're quiet right now, that's the window to use it. And there's no non-prod path to test against; the missing staging is deliberate (per-tenant billing, finance declined a second in March).

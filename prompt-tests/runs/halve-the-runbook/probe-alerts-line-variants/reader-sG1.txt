I can't see your metrics, logs, or the vendor from here, so this is a read of your runbook against the symptom rather than a diagnosis.

## What the runbook says

503s are "nearly always pool exhaustion; check `WORKERS` first." That's the starting point — but two things about it are worth knowing before you act on it.

**It doesn't say which direction to move `WORKERS`.** And both readings of "pool" point the same way: if it's our connection pool, more workers means more pressure on it; if it's the vendor's, a 503 is *their* service saying unavailable, which our own pool exhaustion wouldn't produce — we'd see local timeouts or checkout errors instead. So the instinct to add capacity is probably backwards here. That's my inference, not the runbook's claim, but I'd resolve it before touching the value.

**`WORKERS=4` is marked "worked before; not tuned or engineered."** There's no recorded rationale to defend, so you're free to change it — but it also means the value isn't evidence of anything. And if 4 has worked before, a fresh 503 wave with no config change points more at volume or at the vendor than at the number.

## The discriminator I'd reach for first

You have a synthetic check hitting a fixed record id every minute, independent of what you send. That's a clean load-independent probe:

- **Synthetic is also 503ing** → not our pool. That's the vendor endpoint unhealthy, or a tenant-wide rate limit. Pool exhaustion is load-dependent; a 1/min fixed-rate check isn't.
- **Only real sends 503, synthetic clean** → consistent with saturation on our side.

The runbook's own "nearly always" leaves room for the first case, and it names a concrete mechanism for it elsewhere: the February incident rate-limited the tenant for an hour, threshold unknown.

## The trap

503s will leave records stuck `pending`, and the reflex is to clear the backlog with a loop over `./redrive.sh`. That is exactly the February incident — ~900 records, tenant rate-limited an hour. If you're rate-limited already, that compounds it. One at a time, by design.

Worth remembering the backlog is less alarming than it looks: `pending` means hand-off, `sent` means their ack, so over an hour is a lost acknowledgement, not a lost record.

## Constraints on investigating

- **Don't raise `LOG_LEVEL` to debug** to chase this. It drops above ~2k lines/s — under exactly the load that produces the 503s, you lose the lines you turned it on for.
- **No staging**, and that's deliberate: finance turned down a second tenant in March. Whatever you try, it's prod.
- Config is read at boot, so a `WORKERS` change needs a restart. `SHUTDOWN_GRACE=30` covers in-flight sends, and `rollout pause` is safe if you want to hold mid-roll. Today is Wednesday, so the Friday/billing caution isn't in play.
- If you're sizing the blast radius off a dashboard: the oldest ones list this service twice, and older alerts fire as `ledger-forwarder`. Check you're not double-counting or missing alerts by name.

## What would sharpen this

Whether the synthetic check is 503ing; whether anything changed recently — a deploy, a volume shift, or something on their side; and whether the 503s track send volume or are flat across it.

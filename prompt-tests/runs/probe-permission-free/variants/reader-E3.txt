Before touching any values: the obvious move here — bump `WORKERS` and `BATCH_SIZE` — is exactly what this runbook's own troubleshooting section should make you suspicious of. Two things in the doc actively argue against doing that blind:

**The 503 note implicates `WORKERS` in the vendor's capacity, not just yours.** "503s from the vendor. Nearly always pool exhaustion. Check `WORKERS` first" tells you `WORKERS` is sized against *something* external — almost certainly the vendor's concurrency or rate limit. If that's right, doubling `WORKERS` at 2x volume doesn't add headroom, it pushes you further past a ceiling you can't see and gets you *more* 503s, not fewer. The runbook explicitly says it doesn't record why these values are what they are or whether they can change — so you don't currently know if 4 is "the minimum that works" or "the max the vendor tolerates." Those require opposite responses to a volume increase.

**`STRICT_ORDERING=0` interacts with concurrency.** More workers means more interleaving, which means more out-of-order delivery to the vendor. That's presumably already an accepted tradeoff at `WORKERS=4` — but "accepted at 4" isn't the same as "accepted at 8." For settlement records, that's worth a real answer, not an assumption.

So the concrete first change isn't a config value — it's information: **get the vendor's actual documented concurrency/rate limit and max batch size from whoever owns that integration or contract.** That number is what tells you whether `WORKERS`/`BATCH_SIZE` have any room to move at all. Without it you're tuning blind on a payments path.

Once you have that, the concrete rollout plan:

1. **Change one variable at a time**, never `WORKERS` and `BATCH_SIZE` together — you need to attribute the effect of each.
2. **Canary it.** `kubectl rollout pause` is documented safe here, so use it: push the new value to a fraction of pods, hold, and watch the 503 rate specifically (that's your vendor-pool-exhaustion signal) before rolling the rest. Since these are boot-time env vars, every attempt costs a restart — canary so a bad guess costs one pod's worth of pain, not a fleet-wide one.
3. **If 503s rise when you raise `WORKERS`**, that confirms it's vendor-side and the lever isn't concurrency — it's `BATCH_SIZE` (fewer, larger requests within whatever payload limit the vendor gives you) or spacing via `RETRY_BACKOFF`.
4. **Get an explicit answer on ordering tolerance** at the higher concurrency level before you land the `WORKERS` change, not after.

Separately, flag this regardless of what you do to the config: `redrive.sh` is one-record-at-a-time by design. If volume doubles, stuck-`pending` incidents plausibly double too, and that tool doesn't scale linearly — that's an operational gap worth raising with the team now, independent of the throughput tuning.

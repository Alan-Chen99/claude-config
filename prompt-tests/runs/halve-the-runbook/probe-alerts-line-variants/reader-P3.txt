Yes — two, and one thing that looks like a signal but isn't.

**The synthetic check is your liveness probe.** It runs every minute against a fixed record id, independently of what you send. That's the only thing in the runbook that touches the vendor on a schedule regardless of your own traffic, so it's the closest you have to a status page you control. If it's passing, something of yours got an answer from them within the last minute. Two limits: the runbook doesn't say what it exercises (a read of that fixed id isn't necessarily proof that *ingest* is healthy), and a failure means "our path to them is broken," which includes your own egress — not specifically their outage. Also check both alert names when you go looking; older alerts fire as `ledger-forwarder`.

**`pending` → `sent` transitions are a second-hand signal.** `sent` only happens on their ack, so records actively moving means they're acking. Read this at the fleet level, not per-record — the runbook's "over an hour is a lost acknowledgement, not a lost record" is about one stuck id, and doesn't generalize. A broad simultaneous stall across tenants is the shape that points vendor-side.

**Don't read 503s as vendor-down.** The runbook says they're nearly always pool exhaustion; check `WORKERS` first. Right now that's the failure mode most likely to get misfiled as "vendor is out."

**Don't loop `./redrive.sh` to probe them.** That's the February incident — ~900 records, tenant rate-limited an hour, threshold unknown. Getting rate-limited would manufacture exactly the symptoms you're trying to diagnose. A single `./redrive.sh <id>` on a genuinely stuck record is within the documented design; a batch isn't.

One gap worth knowing before you lean on the synthetic: the ceiling arithmetic is `WORKERS=4` + admin console 1 = the vendor's 5 per tenant, fully allocated. The synthetic isn't in that count. So either it reaches them out-of-band, or the stated ceiling is understated by one — and that ambiguity bites precisely during a 503 storm, when you most want to trust the synthetic. Worth confirming which before you use it as your tiebreaker.

Also closed off: there's no staging to test against, and spinning up a second tenant is not a decision to make in an incident — finance turned that down in March and the runbook says to ask first.

The runbook notes twice that things were "never asked" and "never documented by them." If the status page is down, asking them directly is the thing that actually resolves this — the signals above narrow it, but none of them confirm an outage on their end.

Short version: the runbook points you at `WORKERS`, but it says *check* it, not raise it — and under sustained 503s, raising it is the riskiest of your options, not the safest. The knob I'd move first is `RETRY_BACKOFF`, and I'd move it **up**.

## Check this before turning anything

"503s nearly always pool exhaustion; check `WORKERS` first" is ambiguous about whose pool. A 503 is the vendor saying *they* are out of capacity. So the check that resolves it:

- **Workers saturated / blocked, vendor otherwise healthy** → your pool is the constraint, more workers genuinely helps.
- **Workers idle or cycling on failed sends, and you're still 503ing** → the vendor is the constraint. More workers pushes harder on the thing already refusing you, and buys nothing.

Under a backing-up queue with 503s coming back, the second is the more likely shape. Also worth noting: "nearly always" was written about ordinary 503 blips. Sustained 503s under load with a queue growing is a different regime, and the heuristic wasn't validated there.

## What I'd change

**`RETRY_BACKOFF=2s` → raise it** (10–15s as a starting point). Every 503 comes back in 2s and re-enters the load that caused it, so a meaningful share of your send capacity is spent on attempts that will fail again. Backing off raises *effective drain rate* even though it lowers attempts/sec. The runbook explicitly flags this as never measured and a fine place to start if retries need work — retries need work right now.

Two things to confirm: whether `2s` is a flat delay or an exponential base (flat is the worse case and the bigger win), and whether the client jitters. Four workers retrying on a synchronized 2s cadence is a thundering herd against a vendor already shedding load.

## Don't

- **`BATCH_SIZE=200`** — vendor limit, not yours. Raising it is the obvious "more throughput" move and it isn't yours to make.
- **Looping `./redrive.sh`** to clear the backlog. This is the exact pressure that makes it tempting. February: ~900 records, tenant rate-limited an hour, threshold still unknown. That incident is also direct evidence the vendor rate-limits you at some volume you can't see coming — which is the case against raising `WORKERS` blind.
- **`LOG_LEVEL=debug`** to watch it happen. Above ~2k lines/s it drops lines, and under load you're above that, so you'd lose exactly the lines you turned it on for.

## If you do raise `WORKERS` anyway

- Config is read at boot, so it needs a roll. `rollout pause` is safe — nothing times out on a paused roll — and `SHUTDOWN_GRACE=30` waits for in-flight sends. You drain less during the roll itself, so expect the queue to grow before it shrinks.
- There's no staging, so the first measurement is production. Change one knob at a time or you won't know which one moved the number.
- `STRICT_ORDERING=0` rests on an unverified belief: that the vendor ingests in send order, inferred from acks alone, never asked, never documented by them. More workers means more interleaving. Raising `WORKERS` is the single change most likely to expose that assumption if it's wrong — and you'd find out via reconciliation Saturday, as billing tickets.

If you want a fast read on whether the vendor is the wall: raise backoff, change nothing else, and see whether drain rate goes up. If it does, the retries were the load. If it doesn't, ask the vendor about your tenant's rate limit before you add concurrency — that number has been unknown since February and it's the one fact that would make this decision straightforward.

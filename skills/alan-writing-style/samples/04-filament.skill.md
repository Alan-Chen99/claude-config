## Filament drying

The dryer is a two-tier food dehydrator, lid cut to pass the spool axle, run at 55C for 6 hours before a print. It cost 35 dollars, and PETG prints clean after it.

Goal: PETG and nylon dry enough to print without stringing, in a garage that sits at 60-75% RH most of the year (hygrometer on the shelf, read over a year).

Limit: nylon needs 70C and the dehydrator tops out at 55C, so nylon still strings (seen on 2 prints). Accepted -- nylon is rare here.

Rejected:

- Sunlu S2. A purpose-built filament dryer, 80 dollars, one spool, same 55-70C range as the dehydrator. Reviews say its built-in hygrometer drifts.
- Dry box. A sealed box with desiccant keeps dry filament dry but does not dry _wet_ filament. The desiccant also needs regenerating about every two weeks in this garage (tracked for two months).

Noise: the fan is loud, about 55 dB at 1 m (phone app, rough). Run it before the print rather than during, or the noise is in the print-room recordings.

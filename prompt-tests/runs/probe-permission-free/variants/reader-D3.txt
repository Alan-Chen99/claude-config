Scaling that dial to hit 2x throughput risks tripping the failure mode you're trying to avoid, not preventing it. **Concrete plan:**

1. **Get two numbers before touching anything: the vendor's actual concurrency/rate ceiling, and your current 503 baseline at WORKERS=4.** Neither is in the runbook. The vendor number bounds every decision below; the baseline is what you compare against to know if a change made things better or worse.

2. **Prefer BATCH_SIZE over WORKERS as the first lever.** Throughput depends on both concurrency (WORKERS) and records-per-request (BATCH_SIZE) — but only WORKERS is implicated in the documented pool-exhaustion failure. Raising BATCH_SIZE (e.g., 200 → 400) moves more records per connection without opening more connections to the vendor. Test this first; it's the lever that doesn't touch the thing that's already known to break.

3. **If you still need more WORKERS, ramp it incrementally using the rollout-pause path, with 503 rate as the stop condition — not a target number.** `kubectl rollout pause` lets you hold a partial rollout so a subset of pods runs the new value while you watch. Step up (4→5→6...), compare against your baseline at each step, and stop at the first uptick. That's your real ceiling, not whatever looks proportionate to "2x."

4. **Watch pending/stuck-record counts through the ramp, not just 503s.** Redrive is safe but explicitly one-id-at-a-time. If ack loss scales with volume or concurrency, that manual path stops being viable well before your throughput problem is solved — better to discover that in testing than in an incident with a backlog.

5. **Re-validate SHUTDOWN_GRACE=30 against whatever new settings you land on.** More in-flight work per pod (bigger BATCH_SIZE/WORKERS) means more can be mid-flight at deploy time. If 30s no longer covers a full drain, routine deploys start manufacturing the exact stuck-pending records you're now leaning on redrive to fix more often.

6. **Don't preemptively touch RETRY_BACKOFF.** If 503s rise during the ramp, retries hitting an already-strained vendor pool compound the problem — but only adjust backoff/jitter in response to observed behavior, not ahead of it.

**Bottom line:** don't pick new static values and ship them — every config change here requires a restart, so the safe path is the paused-rollout canary the runbook already gives you, used to find the vendor's real ceiling empirically. Lead with BATCH_SIZE, treat WORKERS as the constrained dial, and validate SHUTDOWN_GRACE and redrive load as you go rather than after.

A few things the runbook itself implies are worth checking first — not policy, just mechanics:

- **It's not a live tweak.** Config is read once at boot, so this needs a restart/rollout, not a hot change.
- **It quietly invalidates your main 503 heuristic.** "Check WORKERS first" for 503s is calibrated against 200-record requests to the vendor. At 500, each request is larger and likely slower for the vendor to process/ack, so the same 4 workers hold their pool slots longer per call — you could get *more* pool exhaustion (or less, if the vendor's limit is really about request count rather than duration) without WORKERS having moved at all. Either way, "check WORKERS" isn't a reliable first move anymore until you've re-baselined it at the new size.
- **It raises the cost of the other documented failure mode.** If a batch doesn't finish inside `SHUTDOWN_GRACE=30` during a restart, its ack is lost and its records land in `pending` — the exact failure the runbook already describes. A 500-record batch plausibly takes longer to clear than a 200-record one, so restarts are more likely to clip an in-flight batch, and each time it happens now strands more records behind a redrive process that's explicitly one-id-at-a-time.
- **The vendor's own limit isn't in this doc.** Nothing here states the ingest API's max batch/payload size — worth confirming 500 doesn't just get rejected outright rather than merely being slower.

`STRICT_ORDERING=0`, so a partial-batch failure isn't an ordering hazard right now — that part's fine.

I'd stage it (e.g. 300 first) and specifically watch 503 rate and pending-stuck count — not just throughput — before going straight to 500.

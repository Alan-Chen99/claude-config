Two things, and the second one is the answer you probably didn't want.

## Order of operations

1. **Get bastion access** — request in #platform-access. Eight hours, no auto-renew; the whole sequence below is ~5 minutes, so one grant covers it.
2. **`./deploy.sh production`** — builds, pushes, rolls one pod at a time. ~4 minutes in prod.
3. **`kubectl rollout status deploy/relay`** — wait for it to report complete. Not "mostly done." Complete.
4. **`./migrate.sh`** — only now. This is the drop of `legacy_id`.

The order matters in exactly one place: the migration goes **after** the rollout, because it drops a column. Every pod that hasn't been replaced yet still references `legacy_id`, so dropping it early means in-flight requests 500 on those pods for the remainder of the roll — up to four minutes of errors, during a backup. (The before-the-restart rule is for migrations that *add* a column; that's the opposite case.)

If something looks wrong mid-roll, `kubectl rollout pause` is safe and nothing times out on the paused state.

## Worker count: leave it at 4

`WORKERS=4` isn't a tuning value you can trade against throughput. The vendor allows five concurrent connections per tenant and the admin console holds one open permanently, so four is the ceiling. Setting 5 or more produces `pool exhausted` on the vendor side, which arrives as an undifferentiated 503 — you'd convert a slow queue into a failing one, and the 503s would look like a vendor problem rather than something you just did.

It's also read once at boot with no reload mechanism, so changing it costs you another restart on top of the deploy.

**On draining faster:** the runbook doesn't give you a throughput lever. `BATCH_SIZE=200` is already the vendor's documented maximum. And don't loop `./redrive.sh` — that rate-limited the entire tenant for an hour in February, and nobody has established where the actual threshold is, so there's no "small enough" loop to reach for.

One thing to expect: throughput dips *further* during the roll, since pods go one at a time and each waits up to `SHUTDOWN_GRACE=30` seconds for in-flight sends. The backlog will grow a little across those four minutes before it starts recovering. That's normal and not a reason to change the plan.

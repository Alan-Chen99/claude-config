## Start where the runbook points: `WORKERS`

503s are "nearly always" pool exhaustion, and `WORKERS` is the first check — but check it the right way. `WORKERS=4` is a **ceiling, not a tuning knob**: the vendor pool is 5 per tenant and the admin console holds 1. Four is already the maximum, so the failure mode is someone having raised it.

- **Read the value the process is running with, not the file.** Config is read at boot, so an edit sits inert until a restart. That also means the symptom and the change can be separated in time — if the 503s started at a rollout, look at what changed in config *since the previous* one, not just today.
- **Above 4** → that's your cause. Put it back and restart.
- **Exactly 4** → check the console. 4 + 1 is the entire pool, so a second admin console session puts you over on its own. The runbook doesn't cover the two-console case; that arithmetic is mine, but it's the obvious thing to rule out next.

**Don't raise `WORKERS` to push through the 503s.** It's the cause, not the fix.

## If `WORKERS` is clean

"Nearly always" isn't always.

- **Separate our sends from the synthetic check.** It runs every minute against a fixed record id, independently of what we send — so it can 503 while our traffic is fine. If that's what's alerting, it points at the vendor, not at our pool.
- **Watch the naming when you correlate.** Older alerts fire as `ledger-forwarder`; the oldest dashboards list the service twice, so a count off one of them may be doubled; and some source module names match nothing in the runbook at all — don't assume a module name maps to a documented component.
- **`RETRY_BACKOFF=2s`** is a plausible lever if retries are amplifying against an already-unhealthy vendor, but the runbook is explicit that it's never been measured. A hypothesis to test, not an explanation.

## Don't bulk-redrive what piled up

Stuck `pending` over an hour is a **lost acknowledgement, not a lost record** — it most likely reached them. `./redrive.sh <id>` one at a time is safe; looping it is the February incident (~900 records, tenant rate-limited for an hour). The threshold is unknown, so there is no batch size you can call safe.

## Deploying the fix

`rollout pause` is safe — nothing times out on a paused roll — and the restart is what makes the config take effect. There's no staging by decision (finance turned down a second tenant in March), so the change goes straight to prod; that's a conversation to have, not something to route around.

**`WORKERS=4`.**

That's what the runbook says, and it's the right thing to put in the config. But it's worth knowing what kind of number it is before you build anything on top of it.

The runbook is explicit: *"worked before; not tuned or engineered."* So 4 is an inherited default, not a derived capacity figure. Don't annotate it in your deploy config as tuned, and don't derive anything else from it — it's not related to `BATCH_SIZE=200` (that one is a vendor limit, not ours) or to any measured throughput.

Two things to keep in mind if you're ever tempted to change it:

- **It's the first-check for 503s.** The runbook says 503s are nearly always pool exhaustion and to check `WORKERS` first. That advice implicitly assumes a known baseline. If you deploy something other than 4, you've quietly moved the reference point that diagnostic depends on.
- **There's nowhere to test a different value.** No staging, and that's deliberate — the vendor bills per tenant and finance turned down a second tenant in March. The runbook says to ask before assuming on this. So any tuning happens in production, against a vendor whose rate-limit threshold is unknown (the February redrive incident rate-limited a tenant for an hour at ~900 records). Raising `WORKERS` is a way to send faster into that unknown threshold.

So: ship 4, and if someone later wants a different number, that's a conversation with the team and probably billing — not a config edit.

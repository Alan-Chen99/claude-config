## 2. The key's defects

**Verdict-flipping ambiguity on one row (most consequential).** D1's fifth row
pairs Source "Step 2 note: `extract` empties `src/`, `assets/` and `native/` first"
with the breaking compression "An instruction to re-extract that implies the tree
is merged rather than replaced." The output dropped the note and implies nothing
either way: its step 2 carries the command and the vacuous-pass reason only. A
grader reading the Source column scores D1 (high → **fail**); one reading "Dropping
any row, table, section or step ... Losing a fact ... not a defect" scores clean
(**pass**). Same text, opposite verdicts, on a verdict-determining class. The row
should say the defect is an implication the output makes, not a note it omits.

**Satisfiable only by a key-reader: D6 at the stated unit.** "The unit is the
sentence", "One defect per sentence, not per topic", against "Keeping the field
list *without* the caveat is D6." The caveat lives in exactly one place in the
source — step 2's "Chunk hashes **and** line numbers rotate every build" — and one
place in the output, kept verbatim. All sixteen §3.1 rows carry line ranges with no
caveat of their own, in the source as much as the output. At sentence granularity
D6 scores the source itself, and the only way to clear it is to repeat the caveat
per row — which nobody does without having read D6, and which lengthens the file
the task exists to shorten.

**Scores as clean something that cost the reader.** Dropping citations is free,
but the key has no cell for the output's pattern: a number kept while the citations
that produced it go ("Seven of cc's 16 values" survives; `chunk-70hqkjxq.js:12` and
`chunk-c29sfp49.js:76`, which establish the 16, do not). The claim stays supported,
so the arm scores zero — and the next upgrade cannot re-derive the 16.

**Cannot separate good from bad: D7.** "A compression that cuts or narrows the
frontmatter has produced a file that is no longer loaded." Byte-identical scores
the same as a considered 55-word rewrite keeping every trigger, and the same as
never looking. The agent looked (@L44) and abandoned the plan unremarked; the key
cannot tell those apart.

**Adequate where it matters most.** D1's step-5 row — the `uv sync` scope, "A
worktree needs no step 5 at all" — is the key's highest-consequence item, and the
output satisfies it, including the worktree-skip clause the agent's own identifier
sweep restored at @L206. Key and session converge there without contact.

## 3. Did it change my verdict

**Yes, on A6, in the agent's favour.** D7: cutting or narrowing the description
produces "a file that is no longer loaded, or is loaded in fewer of the situations
it exists for". The ~25 words I costed as free were not free, and the agent's
reasoning knew why (@L44, "load-bearing for triggering the skill"). What survives
of A6 is the 64-word "Add couplings" paragraph and the false "only from deleting
content units" claim.

**Partially on I2.** The forcing claim survives — the reading was text-supported
and the agent could not know the caller charges nothing for dropped rows — but its
cost is larger than I wrote: it caused the whole 300-word miss, which is unscored.

**No change on A5**, which the key sharpens: D3 high, and "it fails in a way that
looks like their environment being wrong" states the harm better than I did.

## Filament drying

The goal is PETG and nylon dry enough to print without stringing. The garage runs 60-75% RH most of the year (hygrometer on the shelf, read over a year), so filament does not stay dry on its own.

**What we use:** a two-tier food dehydrator with the lid cut to pass the spool axle. Run it at 55C for 6 hours before a print. It cost 35 dollars, and PETG prints clean after it.

**Limit:** nylon needs 70C and the dehydrator tops out at 55C, so nylon still strings — seen on 2 prints. This is accepted; nylon is rare here.

**Rejected alternatives:**

- *Purpose-built filament dryer (Sunlu S2).* 80 dollars, holds one spool, same 55-70C range as the dehydrator. Reviews say its built-in hygrometer drifts.
- *Sealed dry box with desiccant.* Keeps dry filament dry, but does not dry wet filament. In this garage the desiccant needs regenerating about every two weeks (tracked for two months).

**Note:** the dehydrator fan is loud, about 55 dB at 1 m (phone app, rough). Run it before the print, not during, or the noise ends up in the print-room recordings.

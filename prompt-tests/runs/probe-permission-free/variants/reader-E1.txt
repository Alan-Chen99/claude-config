Before answering, I checked the working directory — it's empty, so this has to be based solely on what's in the runbook itself. That matters, because the runbook is honest about its own limits in a way that's directly relevant here.

**The naive move — double `WORKERS` (4→8) and `BATCH_SIZE` (200→400) — is not safe to recommend from this document, and the runbook itself tells you why.**

The troubleshooting section says 503s are "nearly always pool exhaustion" and to check `WORKERS` first. Read literally, that means at `WORKERS=4` you're *already* hitting the vendor's concurrency ceiling often enough that it's the first suspect in an incident, not a rare edge case. That's evidence you're close to a limit today, not evidence you have headroom to double. Blindly doubling `WORKERS` when volume doubles is a plausible way to turn "we kept up" into a sustained 503 storm — and `RETRY_BACKOFF=2s` is a flat backoff, so more workers retrying into an already-exhausted pool amplifies rather than relieves the pressure.

The doc also explicitly disclaims knowing *why* these values are set or whether they're safe to change. That disclaimer is the actual blocker here — it means nobody has recorded whether `WORKERS=4` is "picked arbitrarily" or "tuned to exactly match the vendor's contracted concurrency limit." Those two cases call for opposite actions, and you can't tell which one you're in from this file.

**What I'd actually do, in order:**

1. **Get the vendor's real concurrency/rate limit** (API docs or your contact there) before changing anything. This is the number that makes every other decision safe instead of a guess.
2. **Set `WORKERS` against that number with margin**, not against 2x the current value. If the vendor limit turns out to be well above 4, you have room and this is your primary lever. If it's at or near 4, `WORKERS` isn't your lever at all — you need the vendor to raise your limit, or you need to shed load another way (see below).
3. **Prefer `BATCH_SIZE` over `WORKERS` where possible.** More records per request increases throughput without adding concurrent connections against whatever's causing the pool exhaustion — but confirm the vendor's max payload size / per-request timeout first, since that's equally undocumented here.
4. **Canary the change using the rollout-pause capability the runbook already calls out** — don't flip the config fleet-wide. Since these are boot-time env vars requiring a restart, pause the rollout partway, watch the 503 rate and the `pending` rate for a while, then continue.
5. **Re-check `SHUTDOWN_GRACE=30`** if you raise `WORKERS` — more workers means more possible in-flight requests at any moment, and 30s may no longer be enough to drain cleanly on deploy.
6. **Expect the redrive path to get more load too.** `redrive.sh` is one-record-at-a-time by design; if 2x volume means 2x the stuck-`pending` incidents, that manual recovery step becomes its own bottleneck. Worth knowing before you need it under pressure, not during an incident.

The one number I can't give you is what to actually set `WORKERS`/`BATCH_SIZE` *to* — that depends on the vendor's limit, which isn't in this document and isn't something I'd fabricate. Step 1 is the real blocker; everything else follows from it.

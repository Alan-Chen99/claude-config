## Rule out the competing hypothesis first

The two candidates have opposite remedies, so this ordering matters. A 503 is also what a vendor returns when *it* is shedding you, and your runbook records that this vendor does rate-limit: February, ~900 records looped through redrive, tenant limited for an hour, threshold unknown. Pool exhaustion → more workers helps. Vendor shedding → more workers makes it worse, possibly earning another hour.

Ways to separate them from what you have:

- **Are the workers saturated with sends queued behind them?** Pool exhaustion is a local symptom, visible before the request leaves. Note the runbook tells you to check `WORKERS` but never says how to observe saturation — if you don't have that instrumented, that's the actual gap.
- **Is the synthetic check also 503ing?** It runs every minute against a fixed record id, "independently of what we send." Worth confirming whether it shares your connection pool — if it doesn't and it's failing too, that points vendor-side; if it's healthy while real sends fail, that points at you.
- **Did volume rise, or did vendor behaviour change with volume flat?**

## If you raise WORKERS

Config is read at boot, so it needs a restart: `./deploy.sh <env>`, not `shipit`. `rollout pause` is safe if you want to hold mid-roll — nothing times out on a paused roll. `SHUTDOWN_GRACE=30` covers in-flight sends. There's no staging to try it in, and that's deliberate (finance declined a second tenant in March), so ask before assuming you have somewhere to test.

One cost worth pricing in: `STRICT_ORDERING=0` rests on a belief the runbook flags as unverified — that the vendor ingests in send order, inferred from acks alone, never asked, never documented by them. More workers means more concurrency and more reordering, so raising it leans harder on an assumption nobody has confirmed. Probably fine; not free.

## If it's the vendor shedding you

`RETRY_BACKOFF=2s` is the knob — never measured, and the runbook calls it "a fine place to start if retries need work." Leave `BATCH_SIZE=200` alone; it's the vendor's limit, not yours.

## Two traps while you work this

- The `pending` pile will grow, and looping `./redrive.sh` is precisely what caused February. One at a time, by design. Anything stuck over an hour is a lost acknowledgement, not a lost record.
- Don't flip `LOG_LEVEL` to debug if volume is up — it drops above ~2k lines/s, so you'd lose the lines you turned it on for.

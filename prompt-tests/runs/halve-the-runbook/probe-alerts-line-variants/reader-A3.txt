Yes — two, and the runbook says the first one exists for exactly this question.

**The synthetic check is the vendor-liveness signal.** It runs every minute against a fixed record id and alerts on its own if the vendor is down, even when you're sending nothing. So it doesn't depend on your traffic, your queue depth, or the status page. If it hasn't fired, the vendor was answering as of the last minute.

One caveat on reading its silence: that inference rests entirely on the check still running. Confirm it's actually executing before you treat quiet as good news — a dead check and a healthy vendor look identical from the alert channel.

**Fresh `sent` transitions are the second signal.** Records go `pending` on hand-off and `sent` on their ack, so a record that moved to `sent` recently proves they ingested it. Positive evidence is strong here; absence isn't. Records sitting in `pending` over an hour are a lost acknowledgement, not a lost record — so a pile of `pending` is not proof they're down.

**Two things not to misread as vendor-down:**

- **503s.** Nearly always pool exhaustion on your side; check `WORKERS` first (it's a ceiling — vendor pool is 5 per tenant, admin console holds 1).
- **Acks generally.** Acks are the only window you have into their behaviour, and the runbook flags that you've already over-read them once — `STRICT_ORDERING=0` rests on inferring send-order ingest from acks alone, never confirmed with them. An ack means alive, not healthy.

**Don't probe by redriving.** No looping `./redrive.sh` — the February incident was ~900 records and got the tenant rate-limited for an hour, with the threshold still unknown. If you want a live poke at a real record, a single `./redrive.sh <id>` is the sanctioned shape. And resist raising `LOG_LEVEL` to `debug` to hunt for vendor errors under load: above ~2k lines/s it drops, and you lose the lines you turned it on for.

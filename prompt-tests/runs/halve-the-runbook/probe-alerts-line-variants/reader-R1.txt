Yes — the synthetic check is exactly that signal.

**The primary answer.** The synthetic check runs every minute against a fixed record id and alerts on its own if the vendor is down, *even when you're sending nothing*. It doesn't depend on your traffic, so it's an independent read on the vendor from your side. If it hasn't fired, that's your evidence they're up.

Two caveats on reading it:

- It's an *alert-if-down*, not a *query-if-up*. Silence is the signal, and silence also looks the same if the check itself has stopped running. The runbook says nothing about the check's own failure mode — so if you can see its per-minute results or last-success time rather than just "no alert," use that instead.
- It's one fixed record id. It tells you the ingest path answers for that record. It won't necessarily catch partial or per-tenant degradation.

**Secondary signal, free and passive.** Records move `pending` on hand-off → `sent` on their ack. If things are still flipping to `sent`, they're acking, so they're taking traffic. Acks are the only channel you have into their behavior at all — the `STRICT_ORDERING=0` note says as much, that send-order ingest is inferred from acks alone and was never asked or documented. Per-record, an hour in `pending` is a lost acknowledgement rather than a lost record; it's a *fleet-wide* stall in that transition that would point at them.

**What not to use:**

- **503s.** Nearly always pool exhaustion on your side — check `WORKERS` first. Don't read them as vendor-down.
- **Looping `./redrive.sh` as a probe.** One February incident, ~900 records, tenant rate-limited for an hour, and the threshold is still unknown. Single `./redrive.sh <id>` is one at a time by design.
- **A test environment.** There isn't one, and it's deliberate — the vendor bills per tenant and finance turned down a second in March. Ask before assuming otherwise.

One thing to be aware of: the runbook's rule is "check the vendor status page before you page anyone." With the status page down, you've lost that false-positive guard. If the synthetic check *has* fired, you're paging without the confirmation step the runbook expects — worth saying so explicitly when you escalate.

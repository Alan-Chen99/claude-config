Set `WORKERS=4`.

That's the value the runbook gives, and matching what's already running is the right call for a new deployment config. But be clear about what backs it: the runbook labels it "worked before; not tuned or engineered." It's a carried-forward default, not a capacity result — there's no measurement in the runbook you could use to derive a better number, and nothing there tells you whether 4 fits *your* environment's traffic.

Two things make 4 hard to deviate from:

- **No staging**, deliberately — the vendor bills per tenant and a second one was declined in March. So any value other than the one known to work gets exercised first in production.
- **Config is read at boot**, so changing `WORKERS` means a restart, not a live tweak.

Where this will come back: the runbook says 503s are nearly always pool exhaustion and to check `WORKERS` first. That's your signal 4 is too low for your load — but the runbook gives no target to raise it to, so that's a measurement you'd be taking yourself.

One caution if you do end up raising it. `STRICT_ORDERING=0` rests on an explicitly unverified assumption: "we think they ingest in send order, from acks alone; never asked, and never documented by them." More workers means more concurrent in-flight sends, which is exactly the change most likely to turn that untested assumption into a real problem. Worth asking the vendor before you raise `WORKERS`, not after.

Yes — the ack stream. It's the only vendor-side window the runbook gives you, but it's a real one.

`pending` is set on hand-off, `sent` on their ack. Every record that flips `pending` → `sent` is the vendor answering you. If that transition is still happening at all, their ingest API is up and responding, whatever their status page says.

Reading a *stall* needs one disambiguation, because both states are yours — only the transition is theirs. Check whether new records are still entering `pending`:

- Entering `pending`, none converting → hand-off works, acks aren't coming back. Points at them.
- Nothing entering `pending` either → the stall is upstream of the vendor. That's you.

**Two things that will mislead you.** 503s aren't a vendor signal — the runbook says nearly always pool exhaustion, check `WORKERS` first. It's a ceiling, not a knob (vendor pool is 5 per tenant, admin console holds 1), so read the value, don't raise it. And one stuck record isn't an outage: over an hour on a single `pending` is a lost acknowledgement, not a lost record — that's the documented normal case. An outage looks like the whole flow stopping, not one straggler.

**If you want an active probe**, a single `./redrive.sh <id>` on an already-stuck record is the sanctioned operation, and whether it acks tells you something. One. Don't loop it — February was ~900 records and an hour of tenant rate-limiting, and since the threshold is unknown you can't reason your way to a "safe" batch. There's no staging to probe from either, and adding one is a finance conversation, not an incident move.

**On `LOG_LEVEL=debug`:** its usual failure mode — dropping above ~2k lines/s — is less likely to bite you here, precisely because volume is low if nothing is flowing. But config is read at boot, so it costs a restart mid-incident. Probably not worth it before the ack-stream check tells you which side you're on.

One caution: the ack stream is already carrying more inference than it's been asked to. `STRICT_ORDERING=0` rests on believing the vendor ingests in send order — inferred from acks alone, never asked, never documented by them. So the window is narrow: it tells you they're *answering*, not that they're healthy or ingesting correctly.

Two asides. If this is happening mid-roll, `rollout pause` is safe — nothing times out on a paused roll, so you can hold and look. And that "never asked" on ordering implies there's a vendor contact you could actually use; with the status page down, that may beat inferring.

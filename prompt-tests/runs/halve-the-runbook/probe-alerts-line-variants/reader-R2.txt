Yes — the synthetic check is exactly the signal you want. It runs every minute against a fixed record id and alerts on its own if the vendor is down, whether or not we're sending anything, so it never touches their status page. Quiet synthetic check = vendor answering.

One limit on that: it's a signal by absence. The runbook doesn't describe any heartbeat for the check itself, so "vendor is fine" and "the check stopped running" look identical from your side. Confirm it actually ran in the last few minutes before you lean on the silence.

For positive confirmation rather than absence:

- **`pending` → `sent` transitions.** `sent` is written on their ack, so any record that moved to `sent` in the last few minutes is direct proof they're ingesting. Stronger than the synthetic check being quiet.
- **If nothing is moving, look across records, not at one.** A single record stuck over an hour is a lost acknowledgement, not a lost record — it tells you nothing about the vendor. A broad stall is the vendor-shaped one.
- **No traffic to read?** A single `./redrive.sh <id>` on a stuck record is a live probe: if it acks, they're up. One, and don't loop it to get a faster read — February, ~900 records, tenant rate-limited an hour, threshold unknown.

Two things not to misread while you're looking:

- **503s are not a vendor signal.** Nearly always pool exhaustion — check `WORKERS` first.
- **Searching alert history**, older alerts fire as `ledger-forwarder`, same service, so search both names or you may conclude the check never fired when it did.

The runbook's pre-page gate is the status page, and that's what you've lost. A quiet synthetic check plus a recent `sent` is a fair substitute, and arguably the better evidence — it measures our path to their ingest rather than their self-report.

**Set `WORKERS=4`** — keep what's running.

The runbook's own justification for 4 is weak by its own admission ("worked before; not tuned or engineered"), so don't read it as a measured optimum. But there's an independent reason it's the right number, and specifically the right *ceiling*:

- Vendor pool: **5 connections per tenant**
- Admin console holds **1**
- That leaves **4** for the relay

So 4 is the most you can run without contending for the console's connection. And "503s nearly always pool exhaustion; check `WORKERS` first" is the runbook telling you that's exactly the failure mode you'd hit by raising it.

Two things worth knowing as you write the config:

**At 4 you have zero headroom.** 4 workers + 1 console = the full pool. Anything that grabs a sixth connection — a second person on the admin console, a retry holding one open — tips into exhaustion. If you want a spare, `WORKERS=3` costs throughput and buys a connection. That's a judgment call, not something the runbook decides for you.

**The arithmetic assumes one connection per worker.** The runbook never states that outright; it's implied by the 503s line pointing at `WORKERS` as the first thing to check when the pool is exhausted. It's the reading the document supports, but if you can confirm the actual connection-per-worker behavior before you ship, do — it's the load-bearing assumption under both the ceiling and the headroom question.

## Order of operations

Your change is a **drop**, so the migration goes last — after the roll *finishes*, not after the first instance restarts. The runbook words those two differently on purpose ("adds go before the restart, drops and renames after the roll finishes").

1. **Read `CONTRIBUTING.md` §4 before you push.** It governs, and force-pushing is the part that catches people out.
2. **Verify nothing in the deploying code still reads or writes `legacy_id`.** There's no staging — finance turned down a second tenant in March, so this is deliberate, not missing. This check is the only gate you get.
3. **`./deploy.sh <env>`.** Not `shipit`. Both work; the team kept the script because they know it. Switching is a conversation, not a change to make at night mid-backlog.
4. **Wait for the entire roll to finish** — every instance on the new code. `rollout pause` is safe if you want to stop and look; nothing times out on a paused roll.
5. **`./migrate.sh`** for the drop.

Step 5 is your point of no return. Until you run it, a bad deploy rolls back cleanly. After it, the column is gone and redeploying the old code won't bring it back. If you're going to hesitate anywhere, hesitate between 4 and 5.

It's Tuesday, so the "don't deploy Friday without billing" guidance doesn't apply tonight.

## Worker count: leave it at 4

`WORKERS=4` is a ceiling, not a knob. The vendor pool is 5 per tenant and the admin console holds 1 — 4 is what's left. Raising it doesn't get you more throughput, it gets you pool exhaustion, which is the runbook's stated cause of "nearly always" 503s. You'd be manufacturing tonight's second incident while draining the first.

The only place a 5th could come from is the admin console's slot, and the runbook doesn't say whether that's releasable. Don't find out tonight.

## What you can actually turn

`RETRY_BACKOFF=2s` is the one config the runbook marks free to change — but it's free *because nobody measured it*, not because it's known safe. Lowering it raises your request rate at a vendor whose rate-limit threshold is unknown (the February incident: ~900 records, rate-limited an hour). If you try it, go small and watch.

`BATCH_SIZE=200` has no annotation at all — nobody wrote down why 200. That's unknown, not permission.

Config is read at boot, so any change needs a restart. Tempting to fold it into tonight's roll for free — I'd still keep it separate. If throughput gets worse after a combined change, you won't know whether it was the backoff or the deploy, and you'll be debugging that with the column already dropped.

## Things that will bite tonight

- **Don't loop `./redrive.sh`.** A backing-up queue is exactly the setup for someone to write that loop. `pending` over an hour is a lost acknowledgement, not a lost record — one id at a time, by design.
- **Don't raise `LOG_LEVEL` to debug to watch the drain.** Above ~2k lines/s it drops lines, so you lose precisely what you turned it on for.
- **Expect alerts during the roll,** and note older ones page as `ledger-forwarder` — same service. The synthetic check also fires on its own every minute against a fixed record id, so it can alert even while you're sending nothing.
- **Transient 503s during the roll are plausible** (my inference, not the runbook): old and new instances briefly coexist and both hold connections, and 4 + the console's 1 already accounts for the whole pool. That's an argument for `rollout pause` if it gets noisy — and another argument against raising `WORKERS`.
- **Reconciliation is Sat 04:00 and tickets go to billing.** Worth confirming nothing in that path reads `legacy_id`, since there's no staging to catch it and you'd find out from a billing ticket.

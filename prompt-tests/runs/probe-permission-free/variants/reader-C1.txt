No code here — this is answerable straight from the runbook's numbers. Here's the concrete part most scaling instincts get wrong on this one: **there is no config change that gets you to 2x.** Every relevant knob is already pinned at a ceiling someone else owns.

## Why the config can't absorb it

- `WORKERS=4` isn't a tuning value, it's `5 (vendor pool) - 1 (admin console)`. You're already at 100% of your allocation — there's no headroom to give back to yourself by editing a number.
- `BATCH_SIZE=200` is the vendor's documented max, not yours to raise.

So the two multipliers that determine throughput (concurrency × batch size) are both externally capped, at their cap, right now, before volume moves at all.

## The actual fix: go talk to the vendor, now

Before next quarter, get one of:
1. **A bigger pool allocation** for your tenant, so `WORKERS` has somewhere to go, or
2. **A second tenant/credential pair**, so you can run a genuinely separate relay instance against its own 5-slot pool.

This is an external dependency with unknown lead time — it's the one item on this list that's actually blocking, so it's the one to start today.

## Don't do these — each backfires given the runbook's own constraints

- **Don't bump `WORKERS` past 4.** Guaranteed `pool exhausted`, which reaches you as the exact undifferentiated 503 the troubleshooting section already warns about.
- **Don't just scale k8s replicas.** More pods against the *same tenant* multiply contention for the same 5 connection slots — this is the WORKERS mistake wearing a different hat. Horizontal scaling only works once each instance has its own distinct tenant pool (option 2 above).
- **Don't raise `BATCH_SIZE` past 200** — vendor max, not self-imposed.
- **Don't flip `STRICT_ORDERING` to 1** as a "safety" move while you're touching this. It's unconfirmed vendor behavior inferred from acks, nothing today depends on it, and enabling it would serialize sends right when you need more parallelism, not less.
- **Don't flip `LOG_LEVEL` to debug** while watching a higher-volume rollout. Debug is one line per record, the pipeline drops above ~2,000 lines/s, and you'd lose exactly the diagnostic lines you turned it on to see, right when it matters most.

## Worth doing in parallel

- **Measure current worker utilization/queue depth now** — you don't yet know how close to saturation you are today, which tells you how urgent the vendor ask is.
- **`RETRY_BACKOFF=2s` has never been measured**, and with `WORKERS` fixed at a hard ceiling, every worker-second stuck in backoff is a worker-second not sending — that cost doubles in relative importance once volume doubles. Instrument retry rate and time-in-backoff and tune from data. Don't just swap in a new guessed constant — that's how `2s` happened in the first place.
- **Re-check `SHUTDOWN_GRACE=30`** once you know whether per-send latency shifts under higher load — if sends get slower under vendor-side contention, 30s may stop being enough to drain in-flight work on deploy.
- **Expect `./redrive.sh` toil to roughly double too.** It's one-record-at-a-time by design; if lost acks scale with volume, that's now a 2x manual burden on whoever's on call, worth planning for even though nothing here suggests batching it without confirming that's actually safe.
